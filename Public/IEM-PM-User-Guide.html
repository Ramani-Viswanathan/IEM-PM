IEM-PM

Intelligence Engineering Methodology for Project, Program, Portfolio & PMO

"Intelligence" here isn't a claim about artificial intelligence — it's used the way "business intelligence" is: evidence-based organizational insight built from your own delivery data. The engine that produces it is LLM-native — Claude does the actual reading and judgment, constrained to reason only from the real standards and evidence you provide, never from memory or assumption — while a separate, deterministic layer only validates and renders, never judges. "Engineering" means this is systematic and repeatable, not one-off consulting. And it isn't tied to one PM methodology — IEM-PM audits against whichever real standard your organization actually declares (PMI/PMBOK, PRINCE2, ISO 21502, or your own internal methodology), never a generic one it assumes.

Objective: does the organization's delivery data match what its standards require — and where it doesn't, why? IEM-PM exists to answer that one question with evidence, not opinion — then trace every gap to its root origin so the correction happens at the source, not just in a report.

No dashboard, no login, no server. IEM-PM runs inside Claude Code, reads what you point it at, and hands back one report you open in a browser. This page is the whole manual — for Project Managers, Program Managers, and PMO leads who want to run an audit without touching Python.

First audit: roughly 2–4 hours, most of it Claude reading and reasoning.
Repeat audits: about 30 minutes.

What IEM-PM Is

Discipline, Definition, Problem, Manifesto

Discipline

Project Gap Intelligence Engineering

Definition

The systematic practice of measuring delivery data gaps across project, program, portfolio, and PMO systems, tracing discrepancies to their root origins, and engineering corrections at the source.

Problem Statement

Organizations suffer from compromised strategic decision-making and hidden project failures due to delivery data that is missing, ignored, disconnected, untrusted, underutilized, misclassified, or divergent across PMO systems.

Manifesto

We relentlessly measure missing, ignored, disconnected, untrusted, underutilized, misclassified, and divergent data, and trace it to its root origin — what looks like a delivery problem is often an intelligence-integrity problem.

Prerequisites

Before you start

IEM-PM's one rule is no baseline, no audit — it will not guess at a standard you haven't given it. Everything below has to exist before you ask for an audit.

  • Standards in knowledge/ PMBOK, PRINCE2, ISO 21502, or your own internal methodology — whatever your organization actually follows.
  • A completed PMO Data Charter Start from assets/PMO_DATA_CHARTER_template.md. This is the step that tells IEM-PM what your fields mean — skip it and it will still run, but every finding gets flagged as provisional.
  • Delivery evidence gathered Schedules, RAID logs, status reports, steering packs — whatever you have on hand for the project, program, or portfolio in scope.
  • Claude Code, with this skill available Two ways to get there: open Claude Code inside this cloned repository (works immediately, no install step), or copy/symlink skills/intelligence-engine/ to ~/.claude/skills/intelligence-engine/ once per machine so it's available in every project as /iem-pm:intelligence-engine. Either way, there's no separate app to install.

Nothing you hand it gets changed. IEM-PM only reads your standards, your Charter, and your evidence. The only thing it ever writes is inside your project's own reports/ folder, inside Audit/<YourProjectName>/ — your source data is never touched.

Step by step

The audit workflow

Six stages, always in this order. You only type anything in Steps 2 and 3 — the rest is instructions to Claude Code.

1
You confirm

Standards are in place

Check that knowledge/ actually contains the standards you're auditing against. If it's empty, the audit stops before it starts — that's intentional, not a bug.

2
You write

Complete the Data Charter

Fill in the template: give each artifact an ID (ART-001, ART-002...), define what your fields mean (what does "on track" mean in your steering pack?), and set materiality — what's big enough to matter. This is the only step where your judgment, not the engine's, sets the rules.

Then ratify it — literally type a name, role, and date at the bottom, and save it as Audit/<YourProjectName>/reports/PMO_DATA_CHARTER_ratified.md. That filename is what makes it real rather than a draft.

3
You gather

Assemble delivery evidence

Create a folder for this project under Audit/ — any name you like, e.g. Audit/Acme-Q3-2026/ — and put your schedules, logs, and reports inside an evidence/ folder there: Audit/Acme-Q3-2026/evidence/. Real files, not summaries — the audit cites exact locations, so it needs the originals. Export first if your data lives in Jira, ADO, or Primavera — the engine doesn't log into tools. The engine creates a matching reports/ folder next to your evidence/ folder automatically — you don't create that part yourself.

Before it starts scoring anything, IEM-PM checks whether your evidence set is even enough to support a credible audit. This runs automatically, before Step 4 — you can't skip it and you can't argue with it. It maps your artifacts to 7 categories, then applies 3 rules.

Category Domain Mandatory?
A — AuthorizationCharterRequired
B — ScopeScope2 of B/C/D
C — CostCost2 of B/C/D
D — ScheduleSchedule2 of B/C/D
E — RiskRiskRequired
F — Change ControlChange control—
G — Performance MonitoringReports—
  1. Rule 1 — Mandatory categories. A and E must each have at least one artifact. At least two of B, C, D must also be covered. Miss any of this → the audit halts. No exceptions, nothing you can override.
  2. Rule 2 — Minimum breadth. At least 4 of the 7 categories must be represented overall.
  3. Rule 3 — Minimum artifact count. At least 3 distinct artifacts. One file cannot cover multiple categories by itself, no matter how much it contains.

If your evidence fails this check, you don't get a report. You get a ScopeLimitation_Notice instead — it names exactly which categories are missing and what to add. No score gets calculated on evidence that can't support one.

4
Claude Code runs

Ask Claude Code to run the intelligence-engine skill against your Charter and evidence. It reads everything, compares it to your standards, and writes exactly one file: Audit/<YourProjectName>/reports/IEMPM_AuditGap_Report_DDMMYY_HHMM.md.

5
Claude Code runs

Convert and validate

Ask Claude Code to turn the Manifest into your report — it runs the two conversion steps for you. If you'd rather do it yourself, the commands are underneath.

For reference — two commands, run from skills/intelligence-engine/scripts/ (both scripts default their output next to the input file you give them, inside your project's own reports/ folder, so --output/--output-html/--output-txt are optional): python manifest_to_findings.py --manifest ../../../Audit/<YourProjectName>/reports/IEMPM_AuditGap_Report_DDMMYY_HHMM.md python render.py --canonical ../../../Audit/<YourProjectName>/reports/IEMPM_AuditGap_Report_DDMMYY_HHMM.json
6
You read

Open the report

Inside Audit/<YourProjectName>/reports/ — the rendered .html file. Double-click it, or open it in any browser. That file is the entire interface. Nothing to log into, nothing to configure.

Interpreting the output

Reading your report

The report has ten sections. Most people only need four of them on a first pass — here's what each one is actually telling you.

Section What it tells you Read it when
Executive Summary Total findings, and the Reporting Integrity Score out of 100 — a deterministic score, not an opinion. Always, first
Gap Register Every individual finding: what's wrong, which of the seven gap types it is, and the evidence behind it. Before a gate review
Root Cause Analysis Where each gap actually started — one of seven origins (Capture, Ownership, Tooling, and so on). The cause, not the symptom. When the same issue keeps recurring
Recommended Actions / Roadmap What to fix, grouped by root cause and ordered by severity. Right after the Gap Register
After the report

Remediate, then re-audit

The report is a starting point, not a verdict. A 30-day rhythm that works:

W1

Fix what's Critical or Major

Close every Severity 5 and 4 finding first. These are the ones that threaten delivery outcomes, not just tidiness.

Every Severity 4 or 5 finding now requires your explicit approval before the report can be generated at all — the engine will ask you to confirm it, record your answer as Human Approved, and refuse to produce a report if that field is missing. This isn't a formality: it means a Major or Critical finding is never final on the AI's judgment alone.

W2

Fix the loudest root origin

Look at Root Cause Analysis. If most gaps trace to Ownership, assign owners. If it's Capture, fix the templates. Treat the cause, not each symptom individually.

W3

Close the Missing gaps

Either establish the process the standard actually requires, or update the Charter if it turns out the artifact was never really in scope.

W4

Re-audit with the same Charter

Same Charter, same standards, fresh evidence. The new run's own Date becomes its filename's timestamp (IEMPM_AuditGap_Report_DDMMYY_HHMM.*), so it naturally lands next to the baseline run in the same reports/ folder without overwriting it.

Measure the delta, not just the score. Did the Reporting Integrity Score improve? Did gap density drop? Did the root-origin distribution actually shift, or did the same cause just move to a new artifact?

Who's asking

By role

The workflow above doesn't change. What you do with the result does.

Project Manager

Before your next gate

  • Run it on your own project a few days ahead of a steering committee or stage gate.
  • Use the Gap Register to fix data problems before the meeting, not explain them during it.
  • Treat Recommended Actions as your punch list, not the auditor's opinion of you.
Program Manager

Patterns across projects

  • Run one audit per project in the program, using the same Charter for all of them.
  • Compare Root Cause Analysis across projects — a root origin repeating in most of them is a program-level fix, not five separate ones.
  • Escalate root causes, not individual findings, to your PMO.
PMO

Governance, standardized

  • Own the Charter — write it once, ratify it, and require every audit in the portfolio to use it.
  • Own the standards in knowledge/ so every project is measured against the same baseline.
  • Track the Reporting Integrity Score over time as a governance health metric, not a one-off grade.
Before you ask

Good to know

It stopped and said "no baseline, no audit" — is that broken?
No. It means knowledge/ doesn't have any standards in it yet. Add the PDF or document you actually follow and run it again.
I didn't ratify a Charter — can I still run it?
Yes, but it runs PROVISIONAL: every single finding carries a caveat that it rests on an assumed, not agreed, interpretation of your data. Fine for a first look, not for a governance decision.
Will it change or move any of my files?
No. It only reads your standards, Charter, and evidence, and only ever writes inside your project's own reports/ folder, next to evidence/.
Do I need to learn Python or the terminal?
No — ask Claude Code to run Step 5 for you. The commands exist for anyone who wants to run them by hand, not as a requirement.
Is there a live dashboard I should be looking for?
No, and there isn't meant to be. The rendered IEMPM_AuditGap_Report_DDMMYY_HHMM.html file is the whole interface — open it, read it, share the file itself with whoever needs it.
How do I set this up on a different machine?

You need, once per machine:

  • Git, to clone the repository (or copy the files another way).
  • Python 3.10+, then pip install -r requirements.txt in the repo root.
  • Claude Code, installed and signed in.
  • Your own copy of your organization's real PM standards, dropped into skills/intelligence-engine/knowledge/ — never shipped with the repo.

Then either open Claude Code inside the cloned repository and go, or copy/symlink skills/intelligence-engine/ to ~/.claude/skills/intelligence-engine/ to make it available in every project on that machine, invocable directly as /iem-pm:intelligence-engine. Either way knowledge/ and registries/ stay wherever you put that folder — nothing is copied into a cache, so nothing is at risk when the tool updates.

A fresh clone gives you the engine, not the baseline — knowledge/ and registries/ are gitignored and never travel with the repo. You start with an empty registry on every new machine unless you copy it over by hand; see the next question for what that means in practice.

Does the registry rebuild from scratch every time, or does it get deeper over time?

It gets deeper over time, automatically, with no extra step from you. The first time you run an audit against a standard, Stage 0 scans knowledge/ and builds a shallow, skeleton-level registry from every document's table of contents — enough to know what sections exist, not yet checkable criteria. Then Stage 2 deep-dives only the specific sections that audit's evidence actually cites, derives real checkable criteria from them, and saves the result back into registries/. The next audit that touches the same section reuses what's already there instead of re-deriving it. Run enough audits against enough standards and the registry converges toward full coverage — driven by what you actually audit, not a blind upfront pass over every standard you own.

On a new machine: since registries/ is gitignored, a fresh clone starts with none of that accumulated depth — Stage 0/Stage 2 rebuild it from your knowledge/ standards the same way, audit by audit. If you don't want to rebuild it from zero, copy the registries/ folder itself to the new machine by hand (zip, USB, cloud drive — anything except git), alongside knowledge/. Nothing automatic does that copy for you; it's a deliberate file transfer if you want to carry the depth forward instead of regrowing it.

I'm new — how do I audit a project's artifacts against PMI standards?
  • Create Audit/<ProjectName>/evidence/ and drop your artifacts in (schedules, RAID logs, charters, status reports, etc.).
  • Make sure knowledge/PMI/ has real PMI standard documents in it — nothing runs without a baseline.
  • Open Claude Code, point it at skills/intelligence-engine/SKILL.md, and ask it to audit Audit/<ProjectName>/evidence/ against PMI.
  • Review the Charter it proposes (scope + standards) and ratify, edit, or decline it.
  • Let it run to completion — reports land in Audit/<ProjectName>/reports/.

What it audits: your delivery artifacts against the real PMI standard text you dropped into knowledge/ — clause by clause, never from memory.

Which gaps: one of 7, closed taxonomy — Missing · Ignored · Disconnected · Untrusted · Underutilized · Misclassified · Divergent — each also traced to one of 7 root origins (Capture · Integration · Definition/Taxonomy · Ownership · Process/Cadence · Tooling · Behavior).

What sequence it follows: Baseline → Charter (proposed → ratified) → Define → Measure → Classify → Trace → Engineer & Score → Synthesize (Manifest) → JSON (validated) → Render → Final Summary.

What does "auditing the gap" actually mean — what is it comparing?

Not your project's performance — whether the data PMI requires you to capture and use actually exists, is complete, trusted, and used correctly in your evidence. For every checkable criterion in the standard, it asks "does this evidence satisfy it?" A "no" becomes one of:

  • Missing — required, doesn't exist anywhere in your evidence.
  • Ignored — exists, but isn't acted on.
  • Disconnected — exists, but doesn't tie to another artifact it should reference.
  • Untrusted — exists, but contradicts itself or another source.
  • Underutilized — exists and is correct, but isn't used to inform decisions.
  • Misclassified — exists, but tagged/categorized wrong against the standard's taxonomy.
  • Divergent — exists, but deviates from the standard's defined process/format without documented rationale.

So the check is: standard's required data element → present in evidence? → if yes, is it trustworthy, connected, used, correctly classified, and consistent with the standard's process? Every "no" is one finding, one gap type, traced to a root cause.

What does IEM-PM actually do, end to end?
  • You: create Audit/<Project>/evidence/, drop artifacts in; make sure knowledge/PMI/ has real standard documents.
  • Stage 0 — Baseline: scans knowledge/, builds a skeleton index; halts if empty ("no baseline, no audit").
  • Stage 1 — Charter: proposes scope + standards from the standard, not the data; checksum-checks for a prior audit of the same evidence; you ratify, edit, or decline.
  • Stage 2 — Define: derives checkable registry criteria from the ratified standard, on demand.
  • Stage 3 — Measure: reads evidence artifact by artifact against each criterion; runs four self-consistency sub-checks (same-document header vs. body, cross-document restatement, ID-list completeness, narrative facts) independent of any criterion.
  • Stage 4 — Classify: each unmet criterion becomes one of the 7 gap types.
  • Stage 5 — Trace: each gap is traced to one of the 7 root origins.
  • Stage 6 — Engineer & Score: four-part diagnostic (Evidence · Impact · Why/Who/Scope · Fix-at-source) plus the Reporting Integrity Score (0–100).
  • Stage 7 — Synthesize: writes the Audit Manifest — the LLM↔code boundary. Any Severity 4/5 finding is confirmed with you directly and recorded as Human Approved before the Manifest is written; missing this halts the run.
  • Stage 8 — Findings JSON: manifest_to_findings.py parses and validates the Manifest against the schema.
  • Stage 9 — Render: render.py produces the HTML/TXT reports, byte-identical from the same JSON.
  • Stage 10 — Final Summary: a plain-language wrap-up back to you.
  • Output: everything lands in Audit/<Project>/reports/ — nothing else on disk is touched.
What mandatory evidence is required before it will audit at all?

The Minimum Evidence Sufficiency Gate — 3 cumulative rules, all must pass:

  • Rule 1 — Mandatory, non-waivable: Category A (Authorization) and Category E (Risk) must each have at least one artifact; plus at least 2 of Categories B/C/D (Scope, Cost, Schedule). Miss any of this and it halts immediately, no exceptions.
  • Rule 2 — Minimum breadth: at least 4 of the 7 categories (A–G) must be represented overall.
  • Rule 3 — Minimum artifact count: at least 3 distinct artifacts — one "kitchen-sink" document can't satisfy the gate alone.

The 7 categories (PMI mapping): A — Authorization (Charter) · B — Scope (WBS/baseline) · C — Cost (financial authority/control) · D — Schedule (baselined) · E — Risk (identified/analyzed/owned) · F — Change Control (traceable deviations) · G — Performance Monitoring (actuals vs. baseline).

Fail any rule and there's no Charter, no audit — it writes a Scope Limitation Notice instead (Coverage Analysis table + exactly what's missing), not a partial report.

When will it refuse to audit and give me a Scope Limitation Notice instead?

Only for one specific halt condition — evidence that's readable and ratifiable but too thin. Specifically, when any of the 3 sufficiency rules above fails: mandatory categories missing, fewer than 4 of 7 categories represented, or fewer than 3 distinct artifacts supplied.

Other halt conditions stop the run but do not produce a Scope Limitation Notice — they just report to you directly, no file:

  • No standards in knowledge/ → "no baseline, no audit."
  • Charter proposed but not ratified, and no PROVISIONAL run authorized.
  • No delivery artifacts supplied at all.
  • Every artifact supplied is unreadable (corrupt/encrypted/unsupported).
  • You ask it to modify delivery data — it's read-only.

In short: too thin → Notice; anything else wrong → plain halt, no report artifact.

What can I actually say to Claude Code to start an audit?

A few examples:

  • Full audit, PMI baseline: "Run the intelligence-engine skill (skills/intelligence-engine/SKILL.md) to audit Audit/Acme-Q3-2026/evidence/ against the PMI standards in knowledge/PMI/. Start from Stage 0 and follow the process exactly — including the Minimum Evidence Sufficiency check before proposing a Charter."
  • Provisional/quick-look run: "Run a PROVISIONAL audit of Audit/Acme-Q3-2026/evidence/ against PMI — I understand findings will carry the unratified-Charter caveat. Propose the Charter, then proceed without waiting for me to ratify it."
  • Scoped to specific standards: "Audit Audit/Acme-Q3-2026/evidence/ against only the Risk Management and Scheduling standards in knowledge/PMI/ — propose a Charter scoped to those two before doing anything else."
What artifacts does it deliver after a successful audit?
  • Audit Manifest (.md) — the LLM-written findings, human-readable.
  • Canonical Findings JSON (.json) — schema-validated, machine-readable.
  • HTML report — the executive deliverable.
  • TXT report — plain-text version.

All four share one filename stem (Appendix G naming). If it halts on evidence sufficiency instead, you get a Scope Limitation Notice (.md + .html) — no JSON/TXT, since there's no findings data.

Where are these artifacts stored?
Audit/<ProjectName>/reports/ — always a sibling of Audit/<ProjectName>/evidence/. Gitignored, local-only, one folder per project.
Will it make any changes to my uploaded evidence?
No. Read-only by design (Operating Principle 4). It only reads standards, Charter, and evidence, and only ever writes into your project's own reports/ folder.
What if I don't have PMI standards in knowledge/?
If knowledge/ has zero real standard content of any kind, it halts outright — "no baseline, no audit." But it doesn't have to be PMI specifically — any real standard (PRINCE2, ISO 21502, an Agile framework, your own internal methodology) satisfies the baseline requirement.
Can it audit against other standards? Which ones?
Yes — standard-agnostic by design. Confirmed working today: PMI (PMBOK, Practice Standards, Risk, Governance, etc.), plus Agile and Organizational/internal standards. The evidence-sufficiency mapping also explicitly supports PRINCE2 and ISO 21502, mapped to their own native domain model (e.g., PRINCE2 Themes).
Can I ask it to audit against my own organizational standard?
Yes, fully supported. Drop your org's real methodology document into knowledge/ (e.g. knowledge/Organizational/) and ask it to audit against that. Same 11-stage process — the registry is just derived from your document instead of PMI.
What happens if I ask it to audit with no standards present at all?
It refuses outright — Halt Condition 1. It will not fall back on its own training knowledge or "best guess" at what a standard requires. It stops and tells you to add a baseline first; that's the cornerstone rule, "no baseline, no audit."

If something fails

Symptom Likely cause Fix
BASELINE_ABSENT No standards in knowledge/ Drop your methodology files in and run it again.
Invalid gap_type The Manifest used wording that doesn't match one of the seven gap types exactly Ask Claude to correct the wording — it must match exactly.
Unknown artifact An evidence bullet cites an artifact ID that was never declared in the Charter Add the artifact to the Charter, or remove the bullet.
Claude offers to run the scripts itself A reminder got missed — judgment and execution are meant to stay separate Tell it: "You write the Manifest. I run the scripts."
You get a Scope Limitation Notice, not a report Your evidence set failed the 7-category coverage check (see Step 3) — too thin to support a credible audit Read the notice's Coverage Analysis table, add artifacts for the categories it flags, and re-run.
Never break these

The 5 rules

  1. No ratified Charter, no audit. Never let it guess what your data means.
  2. Every finding needs evidence. No quote, no absence, no finding.
  3. It judges, you run the software. Claude writes the Manifest; you (or Claude, on request) run the two scripts.
  4. Read-only, always. Only the Manifest and your project's own reports/ folder ever get written.
  5. Local-first. No cloud calls, no databases — everything stays on your machine.
